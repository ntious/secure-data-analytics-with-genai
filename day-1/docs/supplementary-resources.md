# Day 1 supplementary resources

These five resources extend the Day 1 topics of AI literacy, responsible use, education, research, copyright, and academic integrity. They were selected for authority, practical relevance, and durability rather than endorsement of a particular commercial AI product.

## 1. UNESCO — Guidance for generative AI in education and research

[Read UNESCO’s guidance for generative AI in education and research](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research?hub=343).

This human-centred global guidance explains how GenAI works, examines privacy and ethical concerns, and proposes approaches for policy, teaching, learning, and research. It is an especially useful companion for discussions about human agency, inclusion, institutional readiness, and the limits of technology-centred solutions.

## 2. NIST — AI Risk Management Framework and Generative AI Profile

[Explore the NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework).

The voluntary NIST framework helps organizations and practitioners identify, assess, govern, and manage AI risks. The page also links to the Generative AI Profile, which adapts the framework to risks that are particularly relevant to generative systems.

## 3. OECD — AI Principles

[Review the OECD AI Principles](https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles).

The OECD principles provide a concise international reference for trustworthy AI, including human rights, transparency, robustness, safety, and accountability. They are useful for comparing responsible-AI claims against recognized values rather than vendor marketing.

## 4. U.S. Copyright Office — Copyright and Artificial Intelligence

[Explore the U.S. Copyright Office’s AI initiative and reports](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/).

This official resource collects reports and policy materials on AI-generated outputs, digital replicas, AI training, and copyright registration. Copyright rules differ by jurisdiction, so participants should treat it as an authoritative U.S. reference rather than universal legal advice.

## 5. Cornell Center for Teaching Innovation — AI and Academic Integrity

[Read Cornell’s AI and academic-integrity guidance](https://teaching.cornell.edu/generative-artificial-intelligence/ai-academic-integrity).

This practical teaching resource addresses transparent course policies, attribution, verification of AI-generated references, authentic assessment, and the limitations of automated AI detectors. Participants should also follow their own institution’s policies and instructor requirements.
